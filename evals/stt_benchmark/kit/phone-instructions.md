# Recording on your phone — step by step

For someone who has never recorded anything before. It takes about twenty minutes in total, including
the ten minutes of speaking.

You need: your own phone, a room you are comfortable speaking in, and the script
(`recording-script.md`). Nothing else — no microphone, no app to install, no quiet studio.

## We want ordinary conditions, not good ones

This is the part people get wrong out of helpfulness. **Do not try to make the recording sound good.**
The product will meet you on your own phone, in your own room, with whatever is happening outside — and
that is the thing we are measuring. A recording made in perfect conditions on borrowed equipment tells
us how a system performs somewhere no candidate will ever be.

So: your normal phone, your normal room, your normal speaking voice and your normal pace. Some traffic
outside, a fan in the corner, a bit of echo — all fine, all wanted.

## Which app

**Use the recorder that came with your phone.**

- **iPhone:** Voice Memos (it is already installed; search for it if you cannot see it).
- **Android:** Recorder, Sound Recorder, Voice Recorder or similar, depending on the make. Most Android
  phones have one. If yours genuinely does not, tell us and we will name one.

Do not change any setting in the app. Whatever it records by default is what we want.

### Not a WhatsApp voice note

Please do not record this as a WhatsApp voice note, and please do not send the files as voice notes.

The reason is technical and it matters: WhatsApp compresses voice notes with its own settings, and **we
compress the audio ourselves**, deliberately, to match exactly what the product receives over a phone
connection. We score both the clean recording and the compressed one. If WhatsApp has already
compressed it, we are measuring WhatsApp's choices on top of ours and neither figure means anything.

## Where to hold the phone

- **About a hand's width from your mouth** — 15 to 20 cm. Not touching your face, not across the room.
- **Hold it still**, or rest it on a table or a book. Do not hold it in the hand you gesture with.
- **Do not cover the microphone.** On most phones it is a small hole on the bottom edge, next to the
  charging port. Check where yours is before you start.
- **Either the phone's own microphone or wired headphones with a microphone is fine** — just use the
  same one for all three parts, and say which you used in Part C.

## What to avoid

These ruin a recording in a way we cannot fix afterwards:

- **A fan or an air conditioner blowing directly onto the microphone.** The room can have a fan; the
  microphone should not be in the draught.
- **Speakerphone, a car kit, or any hands-free set.** They process the audio before it is recorded.
- **A moving car, a bus, a keke.** Road noise plus engine plus wind is not "ordinary conditions", it is
  a different test.
- **Walking around**, or a bag rubbing on the phone.
- **A room with a television or a radio on**, or someone else's conversation in the background — a
  second voice in the audio makes the transcript ambiguous about who said what.
- **Your phone on vibrate on a hard surface.** Put it on silent, not vibrate, or on Do Not Disturb.

## The 30-second test — do this before Part A

**Do not skip this.** The commonest failure by far is a whole session recorded with a covered
microphone or a fan roaring over everything, discovered an hour later, and the only fix is to record it
all again.

1. Start a recording.
2. Say, in your normal voice: _"Testing, one two three. We run the API on Kubernetes and the connection
   pool to PostgreSQL filled up."_
3. Stop it. **Play it back and listen right through, at a normal volume, not on speaker in a noisy
   room.**
4. Ask: can you hear every word clearly? Is your voice louder than the background? Is there any hiss,
   rumble, wind or crackle over the top?
5. If something is wrong, fix it — move the phone, move the fan, close a door — and test again.
6. Delete the test recording. Then start Part A.

## One file per part

Three recordings, three files:

| Part | What                  | File name         |
| ---- | --------------------- | ----------------- |
| A    | The 12 read sentences | `s4-A-read`       |
| B    | The 6 spoken answers  | `s4-B-answers`    |
| C    | The conditions note   | `s4-C-conditions` |

Replace `s4` with **your own speaker code** — the short code we gave you (`s1`, `s2`, `s3`, …). Keep the
dashes and the lowercase code; **do not put your name in a file name.**

If you had to stop and restart inside a part, name the second file `s4-B-answers-2` and send both.

Your phone's recorder will add its own extension — usually `.m4a`, sometimes `.mp3`, `.aac` or `.wav`.
**Any of those is fine and you should not convert anything.** Renaming is usually done by long-pressing
the recording in the app and choosing Rename; if your app will not let you rename, send the files as
they are and tell us which is which.

**Roughly what size to expect:** a few megabytes per part, and under about 20 MB for the whole session.
If your Part A is 40 KB, the recording is nearly empty — check it and do it again. If it is 200 MB, your
phone is recording uncompressed audio, which is fine but awkward to send; tell us and we will send an
upload link.

## Sending them to us

1. **Listen to the beginning and the end of each file first**, for ten seconds each. A file that starts
   mid-sentence or stops early is worth knowing about now.
2. Send all three files to _[upload link or email address we gave you]_.
3. **Send them as files, not as voice notes.** By email, attach them. If you use WhatsApp, use the
   attachment button and choose **Document** — not the microphone button, and not "Audio", which
   re-compresses them.
4. Reply with your speaker code and anything that went wrong or felt odd. If a neighbour started
   drilling in the middle of prompt four, tell us — it is useful, not a problem.

We will confirm we have them and that they play. Please keep your own copies until we do, and then
delete them if you like — they are yours.
