## What a candidate gets that they did not before

They finish an interview and, within about a minute, read a report: a score out of 100, what went
best and what to fix, a breakdown per question with the words they actually said quoted back, what a
strong answer covers, and the criteria the interview never got to — named, and left out of the
arithmetic rather than counted as zeros.

## The five decisions worth knowing

1. **A score is three columns and carries two adjustments, both keyed on facts the candidate can
   check.** ADR-0018, written this milestone, is the assembled answer; `SCORING_VERSION` versions our
   arithmetic rather than the model. The one-line version: a criterion the engine had to *prompt* for
   contributes at 0.85, and a criterion the interview never *asked* about is not assessed at all and
   leaves the denominator. Both are keyed on `session_turns.follow_up_index` — the engine's own
   record, visible in the transcript — never on a model's private verdict.
2. **The report is assembled in code**, not by a second free-form model call, so the strengths and
   fixes are reproducible and cannot flatter.
3. **A person reading a candidate's words is a consented purpose of its own** (ADR-0017), and nothing
   is sampled for calibration except through `ConsentsService.usersGranting("transcript_review")`.
   The tool is built and demonstrated end to end against **staff-written answers only**, because
   `CALIBRATION_ALLOW_CANDIDATE_TRANSCRIPTS` stays false until a signed reviewer agreement exists.
4. **`rejected_criteria` was fixed by a schema, after wording failed twice.** The evaluator's output
   model is built per request from that rubric's own criterion positions, so omitting or inventing a
   criterion is invalid output rather than a gate rejection after the fact.
5. **The engine's reserves are measured now.** `SECONDS_FOR_A_FOLLOW_UP` moved 45 → 75 on the first
   real measurement of how long candidates take, provisionally and on n=8.

## What is not proven, and must not be quoted as if it were

- **The gold set is empty.** Every agreement figure above is against **model-written** expectations:
  a regression baseline, and evidence about drift rather than about quality. `evals/README.md` says
  so above the numbers and `thresholds.yaml` is enforced `on_provenance: human` so it cannot quietly
  start passing.
- **The fairness result is fairness to model-written Nigerian English.** The `nigerian-english`
  answers were written by the same family of model that then scored them. It is the strongest thing
  measurable today and it is not the claim the product makes (product principle 3). Anything
  repeating the figure repeats that clause with it.
- **`SECONDS_FOR_A_FOLLOW_UP = 75` is provisional on eight answers**, with the way to revisit it in
  the constant's own comment.
- **No real candidate has been scored.** Everything here is staff, synthetic answers and the
  stand-in.

## Things that were nearly missed, and what caught them

Worth reading as a group, because none was caught by the thing meant to catch it.

- **A candidate lost 30 points for a question nobody asked them** — caught by reading one paid run's
  report against its own transcript, not by a test. It became decision 3 above.
- **The first paid interview produced no follow-ups at all**, because a dev database had drifted from
  the seed files and the importer's dry run said so and exited 0. `pnpm db:seed -- --check` now has
  an exit code.
- **Two opus runs overlapped**, doubling a bill and exhausting the account's credit mid-run. That is
  the standing rule in CLAUDE.md §7.8, and `--max-cost` now enforces the cap without a person
  watching.
- **A "finding" that was noise.** Strict looked worse than v2 on six rubrics; it was reported with
  the caveat that one run per cell cannot tell a change from a wobble, and the repeat reversed it.
- **A pace report said a session answered 6 questions against a budget of 4** — an *impossible*
  number, which is what exposed three defects in it at once.
- **A test asserted a question's text and matched its probes too**, passing only while selection
  happened to leave that question unprobed.
- **Two screenshots came out byte-identical**, which was the only sign that the capture had
  photographed the wrong page twice.
