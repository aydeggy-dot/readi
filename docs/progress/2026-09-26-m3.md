# M3 — the interview engine, text mode. Handover.

**Branch `feat/m3-interview-engine`, 2026-09-25 → 2026-09-26.** Six phases, two paid runs against
`claude-sonnet-5`, and the milestone the product is named for: a candidate can now sit a typed mock
interview from the web app, be followed up on what they left out, and end it with everything they
said written down.

The plan is `docs/plans/m3-interview-engine.md`; each phase has its own handover in this directory
and this one does not repeat them. What is here is the shape of the milestone, what changed while
it was built, and what the owner needs to know before opening the pull request.

## The three decisions that shaped it

**The engine owns the interview and the model owns the wording.** States, budgets, which probe to
ask and when to stop are code; the model phrases a question, phrases the chosen probe, judges
whether an answer already reached a probe, and answers a candidate's own question. Two paid runs
pushed this *further* rather than back: the intro is now rendered from a template rather than
generated, and the connective between questions is picked by the engine, because both are facts
about the session that a model paraphrases wrongly and cannot remember between independent calls.
The M3 prompt in `docs/PROMPTS.md` said "phrase the intro/transition naturally" and has been
corrected in this change, as CLAUDE.md §7.4 requires.

**The rubric never reaches the interviewer.** A question exists at three widths and
`session-bundle.ts` is the only door between them. The worker gets the prompt, the context, the
probes and `criterion_count` — enough to speak and to log coverage per criterion, not enough to
score. That is a privacy improvement, a smaller injection surface and a cheaper prompt at once, and
`session-bundle.spec.ts` plants a marker in every answer-key field and asserts none come through.

**A session pins what it was run against.** Question and rubric by version *and* snapshot, plus the
catalogue's slugs **and names**, because renaming a role must not rewrite a report the candidate has
already read. `interview-pinning.int.spec.ts` was watched failing twice today, once per half (§4).

## What phase 6 found

Three things, all of them in the test estate rather than the product, and the third one worse than
the two that hid it.

**1. The "known flake" was a real ordering bug.** `interviews-advance.int.spec.ts` occasionally got
`interview_busy` on a plainly sequential request. Closing the SSE stream is what tells the client
the exchange is over, and the lock was released on the line *after*. A client that sends its next
answer the moment the stream closes — a script, a test, M5's LiveKit agent — met a lock the exchange
had already finished with. Releasing before closing inverts the race and is strictly safer, since
everything the exchange writes is written before the `finally` runs. Three consecutive whole-suite
runs are clean. It was carried as "a fix for the fixture" in two handovers; it was four lines in
`interview-advance.service.ts`.

**2. The e2e interview spec has to own its content.** The first version interviewed for
`backend`/`mid` off the shipped bank, and its follow-up-cap assertion passed and failed at random:
`content.spec.ts` publishes a question into that exact pair on every run, with no planned
follow-ups, and the e2e database is never reset. A question with no probes can never produce a
follow-up. The spec now seeds and publishes its own role with four questions of two probes each,
which is the only way "answering incompletely earns exactly two follow-ups, and a third answer moves
on" means anything.

**3. In a fresh e2e database, no question is published at all.** The importer never publishes
(ADR-0014 decision 5) and `catalogue.setup.ts` publishes only the catalogue. Nothing had noticed
because `content.spec.ts`'s leftovers were always there — which is also why the first interview
screenshots came out full of "A report page takes \*\*nine seconds\*\* to load … d2571bd2", literal
asterisks and all. `publishSeededQuestions` in `e2e/helpers.ts` now publishes a few *general*
questions of the shipped bank for a role and level, and the visual capture uses it, so what is
photographed is the bank. Recorded in CLAUDE.md, because it will catch the next person too.

## The e2e database now starts empty, and that found a fourth thing

Three findings in a row came from one cause, so the cause was fixed rather than each symptom:
**`e2e-prepare.ts` drops and recreates the database** instead of creating it if missing. It costs
nothing measurable — the whole suite is 2m57s from a cold database against 55s warm, and most of
that gap is the first Prisma migration, not the reset — and it buys failures that mean something.

It is guarded. The command refuses any database whose name does not end in `_e2e`, before it opens
a connection, and says which one it refused; nothing else in the repository drops anything. Checked
by pointing it at `readi_guard_check`, which it declined and did not create. `WITH (FORCE)` closes
connections an interrupted run left behind, because "database is being accessed by other users" is
a confusing way to learn that.

**The first run on an empty database failed immediately, and on something real.**
`onboarding.spec.ts` selected the stack radio "Go" for a Backend engineer. Backend has offered four
variants since the backend blueprint took `golang`, `dotnet` and `ruby-rails` off `roles.yaml` — so
that line had been fiction for two milestones, and it passed only because the never-dropped database
still held the old `career_role_stacks` row. `capture.spec.ts` had the same line. Both now pick
Java / Spring.

That is the M2.5 lesson arriving a second time in a form a grep could not have caught: the field was
not renamed, the *content behind it* was withdrawn, and only a database that starts empty can tell
the difference. The suite is green from nothing: 9 passed, and 152 screenshots re-captured.

## The verifications the plan asked for

**The pinning test watched to fail, both halves** (the M2 lesson). Two mutations in the production
code, each reverted afterwards:

- reading the live question row instead of the pinned snapshot →
  `expected 'EDITED prompt: this is not what the c…' to be 'VISIBLE prompt cb0fd646: …'`
- reading the live role name instead of `interview_sessions.catalogue` →
  `expected 'RENAMED role' to be 'Fixture role 2080dfed'`

Nothing else in the suite noticed either one, which is the point of having the test.

**A Redis flush mid-session, then a resume** — and it is now a permanent test rather than a
one-off. The e2e spec deletes `interview:<id>` from the worker's Redis between two answers and
carries on; the API log shows `worker asked for the bundle again` and the candidate sees nothing.
That is the whole claim that the worker's Redis is a cache and the API's snapshot is the authority.

**A question the session has not reached is never sent to the browser** — asserted against the page
that was rendered *before* it was asked, and extended to its probes, which are answer key right up
until an interviewer speaks them.

**Slow 4G**, on this machine, over loopback and uncompressed:

| page | load | weight |
|---|---|---|
| landing | 2766 ms | 247 KB |
| sign-up | 3296 ms | 289 KB |
| log in | 3295 ms | 288 KB |
| CMS question list | 2869 ms | 279 KB |
| CMS question form | — | 310 KB |
| Practice list | 2873 ms | 267 KB |
| **interview screen** | 1221 ms | **273 KB** |

All well inside the 900 KB ceiling. Two of the timings are not to be trusted and the numbers are
the useful part anyway: the CMS form reported a *negative* duration this run, which is a
pre-existing artefact of `measure()` on a navigation the browser had already prefetched, and the
interview screen's 1221 ms is the same effect in a milder form. Worth fixing in the helper when
somebody next touches that spec; it does not change what the weights say.

**152 screenshots** in `screenshots/m3/` (gitignored), 38 screens × 2 widths × 2 themes, including
the four new ones: the Practice list with one interview running and one finished, the setup screen,
the interview itself and the completion screen.

## One copy fix, found by looking at the pictures

"You answered 1 of 4 questions in **1 minutes**." `interview.complete.leadOneMinute` is a second
variant rather than a plural engine, because this is the only counted noun in the product whose
value can be one.

## What is not done, and is not hidden

- **Langfuse is off.** Built, masked, deletable, tested disabled — and unverified against the real
  service, because there are no keys. ADR-0008's "verify retention and bulk trace deletion in M3"
  therefore stays open; `docs/runbooks/langfuse-enable.md` is the ten minutes it takes once keys
  exist.
- **Scoring is M4**, and every screen that could imply otherwise says so in the candidate's own
  language rather than in a disabled button.
- **The coding round is [P2]** (owner's decision, 2026-09-25). The setup screen and the completion
  screen both say which round this is and which two it is not.
- **`weak_topics` is empty** until M4 has evaluations to derive it from. The input exists and is
  wired; only the data is missing.
- **The Slow 4G helper's timings**, which can be negative on a prefetched route. The weights are
  measured differently and are unaffected, so the budgets still mean something; it is the printed
  numbers that mislead. Carried in `tasks/todo.md` for whoever next touches that spec.
