# M4 phase 5 — the eval harness, and the caching the model comparison needs

**2026-09-28, branch `feat/m4-evaluation`.** The harness is built and proved on the stand-in
evaluator. **Nothing has been spent yet**: the paid run is waiting on the owner's go-ahead, and the
numbers to give it on are at the bottom of this page.

The owner asked for two results above everything else: whether the evaluator keeps the
`nigerian-english` answers within one point of `strong` (a bias against our core users, and the top
finding if it fails), and a sonnet-5 against opus-5 comparison on one stratified sample with evaluator
prompt caching applied. This page is what was built to answer them, and one thing that had to be
decided before either could be measured.

---

## 1. The harness

`cd apps/ai-worker && uv run python -m readi_worker.evals.run`. It reads `content/seed` and
`evals/datasets` and needs **no database**, no Redis and no service token — which is what makes it
runnable in CI, on a laptop and from a `workflow_dispatch` job. `evals/README.md` is what the numbers
mean; `readi_worker/evals/__init__.py` is why it is shaped this way.

Every call goes through the same `EvaluationService` the worker serves, over the same
`EvaluateAnswerRequest` contract the API sends. That is the point of not rendering prompts directly:
the harness measures the three gates, the retry loop and the evidence verifier that actually run, and
a harness that assembled its own prompt would drift from production in exactly the way that makes an
eval reassuring and useless.

### What it measures, in the order the report prints

**Fairness, first and outranking the rest.** `nigerian-english` says the same engineering as `strong`
in the idiom a Nigerian candidate actually uses. Every criterion where it scores more than one rung
below `strong` is a descriptor rewarding a particular English. It is measured **per criterion, with
the dimension named**, because a weighted average hides the one descriptor that cost three rungs
behind two that cost nothing — and the criterion is the thing you would change. The band is
**one-sided**: the idiom scoring *higher* is noise in a direction nobody is harmed by, reported and
never failed on.

**The two separations**, on the rubric's weighted 0-to-4 score with `check-stress.mjs`'s 0.8 margin.
Deliberately the same constant, so "the rubric separates" and "the model separates" are read on one
axis: that script says whether the rubric can tell confident-and-wrong from right, and this says
whether the model does, and only both together are worth anything.

**And the fairness result is not proof, however it comes out.** The `nigerian-english` answers are
**AI-written** — a model's idea of the idiom, scored by the same family of model. A passing band shows
the evaluator is fair to *that*, which is the strongest thing measurable today and is not the claim the
product makes (product principle 3). Two ways it passes and is still wrong: the drafter wrote a milder
idiom than candidates actually use, so the descriptors that would punish real speech are never tested;
and a model may read its own register more charitably than a person's, which no amount of sampling from
this set can detect. Validating it against real answers needs `transcript_review` consent, a signed
reviewer agreement and a route into `evals/datasets/gold/` — so it sits behind phase 6 and the pilot.
The format, the loader and `--dataset gold` are already in place; it is the answers that are missing.

**Agreement**, per criterion, as exact match, within-one, MAE, correlation and bias. Five figures
because each hides what the others show — exact match is brutal on a five-rung ladder, within-one
flatters, MAE is blind to direction, and correlation is blind to a model that is uniformly two rungs
generous, which only `bias` sees.

### The thing that keeps agreement honest

Against `evals/datasets/synthetic` the agreement figures are a **regression baseline and nothing
else**. Those 510 expected scores were written by a model, so agreement with them says whether a fixed
answer's score has *moved* — worth knowing, and the whole reason to keep them — and says nothing about
whether a score is right. `Dataset.provenance` carries that fact from the files into the report, which
prints the caveat above the numbers rather than in a footnote, and `evals/thresholds.yaml` enforces the
agreement thresholds only `on_provenance: human`.

`evals/datasets/gold/` now has its format, a worked `*.template.yaml` the loader skips, and a README
that says plainly that it is empty and why nothing may be moved across from `synthetic/`.

### The strongest test in the phase

`test_the_written_scores_pass_their_own_checks` feeds the corpus's **own** expected scores through the
harness and requires the same verdict `check-stress.mjs` reaches on the same numbers: 102 rubrics, no
problems, perfect self-agreement, every fairness drift inside the band. Two implementations of one rule
in two languages, agreeing on the real corpus — the arrangement `ask-vectors.json` already uses to keep
the two ask-counters from drifting. It caught nothing, which is the point: it is what makes the Python
figures comparable to the ones the bank has been held to since M3.

### Sampling

A sample has to be **whole rubrics**, never loose answers: the fairness band and both separations
compare one rubric's five answers with each other, so sixty answers picked individually would measure
none of them. `stratified_sample` round-robins over (role, question type, level) strata so that forty
frontend technical rubrics cannot crowd out the rest, and is deterministic in `--seed`. Twelve rubrics
is sixty answers, which is the size the owner approved (M4 decision 9).

### `--smoke`, and what it refuses to assert

`--smoke` runs the stand-in evaluator over two rubrics with no key and no cost, and it is inside
`pnpm test` (`tests/test_evals_run.py`) for the M2.5 reason: a test that only runs when asked is a test
that rots, and this harness is otherwise dispatched by hand.

It checks the **machinery** — every answer came back with one score per criterion, every non-zero
criterion carries a verified quote (spec §6.2), nothing was billed — and it deliberately does **not**
check the fairness band or the separations, because the stand-in scores on whether the word "because"
appears in the answer. A green separation against that would be a coin toss with a tick beside it, and
that is worse than no harness. The §6.2 check was watched failing, by stripping the quote counts.

`.github/workflows/evals.yml` is `workflow_dispatch` only, with the path filter that would make it
automatic written out and commented, and the reason in the file: a paid run is $1–3, so it cannot hang
off `push` without spending money on every commit that touches a comment.

## 2. Prompt caching, which the comparison could not be done without

The owner asked for the comparison "with caching applied". Applying it turned out to require a decision
rather than a flag, and the phase 4.6 write-up's preferred remedy is not available to us.

### The seam

`LLMClient.parse` gains `cache_system`, and the Anthropic client sends the system prompt as a single
block with `cache_control: {type: ephemeral}`. One breakpoint, on the system block, **explicitly** —
not top-level automatic caching, which would place it after the per-call tail and write an entry no
later call could read: a pure surcharge whose signature is a cache write on every request and a read on
none. The cacheable prefix is the system prompt and nothing else, because render order is
`tools → system → messages` and this call's user message diverges at its first interpolation two lines
in. Measured: 6,281 characters, ~1,698 tokens — above opus-5's 512-token minimum and sonnet-5's 1,024,
and a test holds it there because phase 7 may switch models and a prompt under the minimum caches
**silently not at all**.

### The cost has to stay derivable from its own row

`LLMResult`, `AiCallRecord` and `ai_call_log` gained `cache_write_units` and `cache_read_units`, kept
apart from `input_units` (the uncached remainder, which is what the provider reports). `pricing.py`
prices them at 1.25× and 0.1× of the input rate. Folded together, a cached call would look four times
cheaper than it was — and a cost measurement built on a cost field that lies is worth nothing, which
is the entire reason this phase touched the schema at all. The asymmetry across the boundary is
deliberate: required in the generated Pydantic, so the worker always sends them; defaulted in Zod, so
an API a deploy ahead of its worker does not 500 over two integers that are almost always zero.

### The fan-out had to lose its first place

**Four parallel calls on a cold prefix are worse than not caching at all.** A cache entry can only be
read once the request that wrote it has answered, so `EVALUATION_CONCURRENCY = 4` means four writes at
1.25× and no reads: `4 × 1.25` against the `4 × 1.00` of sending it uncached. At MVP volume the cold
prefix is the normal case rather than the edge one, so shipping caching without touching the fan-out
would have *raised* the bill.

**And pre-warming, which the phase 4.6 write-up preferred, is not open to us**: `max_tokens: 0` is an
`invalid_request_error` together with `output_config.format`, and every evaluator call is structured
output. Recorded in `evaluation/calls.py` so nobody spends an afternoon rediscovering it.

So the processor scores the **first answer alone** and fans the rest out behind it. One call's latency
— ≈15 s measured — takes a 4-answer session from ≈15 s to ≈30 s and an 8-answer one from ≈30 s to
≈45 s, both inside spec §8's 60 s. `evaluation.processor.spec.ts` pins it, **watched failing** with the
old shape put back, and also pins that the tail really is concurrent, because a test that passed on a
wholly sequential loop would be worthless.

What it buys, on a 4-answer session: the prefix costs 0.85¢ a call on opus, so four uncached calls pay
3.4¢ against 1.32¢ with one write and three reads — **2.1¢, about 9% of the evaluation bill**, cold.
Warm it is better and the entry is **global**: the system prompt is identical for every answer of every
session of every candidate, so under any traffic at all the write is somebody else's and every call is
a read.

**Caching has not yet been observed working against the real API.** `cache_read_input_tokens > 0` is
the only ground truth, and it needs a paid call. The report says so loudly if it ever asks for caching
and reads nothing back, naming the three things that cause it — so the first paid run is also the
verification of this.

## 3. What the paid run will cost — the numbers to approve

`--dry-run` prints this, and it will print a **counted** input figure rather than an estimated one when
a key is in the environment (`messages.count_tokens` is free; `count_input_tokens` is tested against a
mock transport). The figures below are from a run without a key, so the input side is estimated at
3.7 characters per token; the phase 4.6 sensitivity analysis puts that band at 11–13%.

**The sample: 12 rubrics × 5 answers = 60 answers**, `--seed 7`, spread over three roles, four
question types and both levels:

| rubric | role | type | levels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ambiguous-work-judgement` | backend | scenario | intern-junior, mid |
| `async-ordering-understanding` | frontend | technical | mid |
| `async-unblocking` | frontend | scenario | intern-junior, mid |
| `client-boundary-reasoning` | frontend | technical | intern-junior, mid |
| `help-seeking-judgement` | frontend | behavioral | intern-junior |
| `incident-ownership` | backend | behavioral | mid |
| `raising-a-quality-concern` | qa | behavioral | intern-junior, mid |
| `scaling-out-reasoning` | backend | scenario | mid |
| `stale-write-diagnosis` | backend | technical | intern-junior, mid |
| `test-data-judgement` | qa | scenario | intern-junior, mid |
| `transaction-boundary-reasoning` | backend | technical | mid |
| `unfamiliar-code-approach` | backend | behavioral | intern-junior, mid |

174,157 input tokens and ~60,000 output (1,000 a call, the first paid run's measured average — the one
estimate here, because there is no free way to know in advance how much a model will write):

| | no caching | cached, sequential | worst case (+20% retries) |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| `claude-opus-5` | $2.37 | **$1.92** | $2.31 |
| `claude-sonnet-5` | $0.95 | **$0.77** | $0.92 |
| both | $3.32 | **$2.69** | **$3.23** |

The 20% is the rejected-reading rate of the first paid run — one call in five bought nothing, which was
the gates working, and it belongs in every estimate rather than in the surprise afterwards.

Two notes on the shape of the run. It is **sequential**, which is what makes the cache pay and the bill
predictable; `--concurrency 4` would turn the first four reads into four writes. And it writes
`evals/results/<timestamp>-<model>.json` holding every per-criterion score and every call's usage, from
which every figure in every report — including the cross-model comparison, which is free and
repeatable — is recomputed. A run happens once; its numbers have to be re-cuttable for ever, including
against a gold set that does not exist yet.

## What is verified

- `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck` clean. `pnpm test` green: **599** API, **396** worker, 176 web, 119
  shared-types, 56 ui, 3 api-client.
- `pnpm check:contracts` regenerates cleanly; it reports "out of date" only because the generated files
  are not yet committed, which is what that gate is for.
- The migration was read by hand: Prisma proposed `DROP INDEX "questions_embedding_hnsw"` for the
  **eleventh** time, in a migration that adds two integer columns to `ai_call_log` and does not touch
  `questions`. Deleted, and applied with `migrate deploy`. Nothing was added to `HAND_WRITTEN_SQL` —
  this migration creates no hand-written object.
- Two behaviours watched failing and then green: the fan-out's head (`evaluation.processor.spec.ts`,
  with the old `concurrently(all)` put back) and the smoke run's §6.2 evidence check (with the quote
  counts stripped).
- Not run: `pnpm test:e2e`. Nothing in this phase touches a browser path, and a build would write
  `apps/web/.next`. The e2e interview → report spec is still owed by phase 7.
- **The intermittent API failure was caught and named on the way past** — twenty runs had not
  reproduced it, and the log being saved rather than tailed this time is what did it
  (`2026-09-28-flaky-test-hunt.md`, appended). It is `test/content-no-answer-key.int.spec.ts`, and it
  is a **Postgres connection timeout in `beforeAll`**, not an assertion: a Failed Suite, which is why
  the counts read `568 passed | 31 skipped` and a grep for failed *tests* saw nothing.

  **The second error was ours and hid the first.** That spec's `afterAll` ran
  `deleteMany({ userId: { in: [undefined, undefined, undefined] } })` — a `beforeAll` that dies before
  the three `giveProfile` calls leaves every id unset — so a confident, specific, wholly derivative
  `PrismaClientValidationError` is what the log showed. Fixed: the hook filters the ids it has, skips
  content it never created and closes an app that may not exist. The contention itself is deliberately
  not fixed — a pool timeout under six parallel package suites on six cores and 7 GB is the machine,
  and every remedy trades something real against a failure seen once in twenty local runs and never in
  CI. What changed is that it names itself on the first occurrence now.

## What is not done, and is the owner's call

- **The paid run.** Waiting on the go-ahead. Both models, 60 answers each, ≈$3.23 worst case.
- **The recommendation** that decision 7 asks for, which cannot be written before the run. What the
  harness will produce is: each model's fairness verdict, each model's separations, agreement between
  the two and of each against the written expectations, and the cost per answer and per session — and
  the recommendation follows from those in that order, because product principle 1 puts the quality of
  the feedback above what it costs.
- **Fairness against real candidates**, which is what would make the result proof rather than the best
  available measurement. It needs consented pilot answers in `evals/datasets/gold/` and therefore sits
  behind phase 6; until then the figure is quoted as "fair to model-written Nigerian English" and never
  as "fair" (`tasks/todo.md`).
- **The interview's own caching**, which is the larger prize and is untouched. Its fourteen calls in a
  session share both a system prompt *and* a session bundle, which is a per-session prefix worth far
  more than a per-call one, and the interview is sequential by construction so it would need no
  reshaping at all. It is only 4.79¢ to save 12–16% of, so it is not urgent — but it is the same
  mechanism and the seam is now in place.
