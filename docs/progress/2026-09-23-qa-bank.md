file is `status: draft` / `author: ai_draft`, so production refuses to publish any of it until a human

## Why QA was different from the other two

The catalogue rates QA a **full** fit for this engine — no missing round, no tooling owed — so for
this role the bank is the whole product. It is weighted accordingly: **25 of the 35 questions are
`test_design` or `scenario`**, and nothing asks a candidate to write code. It is also the only bank
where the borrowed questions outnumbered the role's own before this pass: nine of the twelve a QA
candidate saw were judgement questions from the frontend and backend banks.

## The two mechanical findings, and why they matter beyond QA

Both came from one critique pass and both were verified in a single command before anything changed.

1. **The weights were a template, not a claim.** 21 of 30 rubrics were exactly 35/35/30 and 8 were
   35/30/35. `REVIEW.md` tells the expert that weights "are a claim about what matters most" — these
   asserted nothing, so the criterion carrying the judgement that transfers was the lightest by
   default, because it was written last. The comparison is what makes it damning: **frontend has 11
   distinct patterns across 30 rubrics, backend 12 across 29.** Now 13. `check-bank.mjs` warns when one
   split covers more than half a rubric file.
2. **The manner defect returned for a third time, in a form no lexical check covered** — the wrong
   answer defined by the *quantity* of speech ("a detailed plan", "a thorough set of flows", "Prices it
   accurately"). Nine in QA, and the new check immediately found **seven more in the frontend, backend
   and shared files**, so it was never a QA problem. All sixteen fixed. `detailed`, `in detail`,
   `thorough*`, `accurately` and `at length` are now on the suspect list.

And the fairness pass's central finding, which is the one to carry to every future bank:
**`qa/rubrics.yaml` contained not one clause protecting a candidate with no workplace, access or
colleagues**, while `rubrics.shared.yaml` carries eleven. The bank's whole fairness promise — an
answer from a personal project scores the same as one from a job — sat in a YAML comment at the top of
`questions.yaml` that said, in so many words, that the seed format had nowhere to put it. It has
somewhere: the descriptor. Ten criteria now carry it. **A promise the model cannot read is not a
promise.**

## The fact-check: three of eight claims had moved

Versions: Cypress 16.1, Playwright 1.63, Selenium 4.49, REST Assured 6.0.1, Appium 3.7, Postman v12.

- **`selectors-that-break` was wrong for two of its three tagged stacks.** "Find it by accessible role
  and visible text" is Playwright doctrine. **Cypress 16 ships no role or label query at all** and its
  own Best Practices rates a `data-*` attribute "Always"; Selenium has no role locator. The answer key
  and rubric are now tool-conditional.
- **Newman no longer reads the collection format Postman v12 uses** — the current tool is the Postman
  CLI. Nothing in the question names a tool, so a candidate answering "newman" is a version behind
  rather than wrong.
- **Postman's secrets vocabulary moved** — no "secret" variable *type*; it is a **secure variable**,
  with vault secrets as the recommended home.

Three numeric defects were found by the **arithmetic** pass, not the fact-check: a "one in twenty"
written as "twenty-something in twenty", whole-naira boundaries on a field that takes kobo, and a
photographed CV described as "several megabytes" when the point is that it passes a 5 MB limit.

## The stress test: 165 answers, one hard failure, and a defect in my own prose

Written blind by seven subagents, scored by seven more. **Every rubric passes, and
`nigerian-english` scored identically to `strong` on every criterion of every rubric — zero drift in
99 criteria.**

- **`assertion-strength` failed at +0.60**, for the reason its own `reviewer_notes` had already
  predicted: the context hands the candidate the fact criterion 1 was worth 40% for noticing. A `weak`
  answer read the line back and scored 3. Criterion 1 now puts the premise-read-back at level 2.
- **24 wrong beliefs had no descriptor to land on.** Two scorers named the same shape independently:
  *the level-1 descriptor was written for a wrong answer, not for **this** wrong answer.* Three times
  the wrong belief scored 3 on the heaviest criterion.
- **Two of my own rubrics had levels 1 and 2 that were not on one axis**, so an answer doing both
  fitted neither.
- **`api-collection-repeatability` required something the question never said** — "the token expires"
  at level 3, against a context with no token lifetime.
- **And a false claim in my own note.** Of `testing-without-a-spec` I had written that `03/11/2026` and
  `11 March 2026` "cannot both be right". They can — same date, month first. A blind writer found it by
  taking the same wrong turn in the other direction. The question is better than I described it (it is
  about which convention the product means, and dd/mm is the local one), but the note and one
  answer-key point described a question that had not been written.

`check-stress.mjs` also had a latent crash: it built its failure messages with a `const fmt` declared
*after* the loop that used them, so the first separation ever to fail crashed instead of reporting.
Fixed.

## Open for the owner

`tasks/todo.md` § "Question banks — QA" carries the list. The first two are the ones that change
things beyond this bank:

1. **The triple-barrelled prompt.** Two passes made it their first finding, and the argument that
   matters is that asking all three clauses up front **pre-empts the engine**, whose job is follow-ups
   probing missing rubric points (CLAUDE.md §5). Against it: the prompt-clause rule, written because
   the frontend pass found nine criteria charging for something never asked and backend eighteen, and
   enforced by `check-bank.mjs`. Not resolved inside one bank.
2. **`behavioural-answer-quality` now has no question in any bank.** Four have used it; all four needed
   their own. Delete, or keep as the starting point a new role is expected to outgrow?
3. **`manual-exploratory` is the default QA variant with zero tagged questions** — the blueprint's
   argument holds, but the most common candidate in this market practises nothing about their own
   working week.
4. **Android on a real phone, as general content** — the largest gap in the bank.
5. Two accepted shortfalls, reported every run: `test-automation` @ intern-junior (1 of 2) and
   `performance-testing` (1 of 2).

## Running it again

```bash
node .claude/skills/question-bank/scripts/check-bank.mjs            # house style, targets, weights, manner
node .claude/skills/question-bank/scripts/check-bank.mjs --numbers  # the arithmetic worklist
node .claude/skills/question-bank/scripts/check-stress.mjs          # 99 rubrics, both separations
pnpm db:seed -- --dry-run                                           # the contract
pnpm --filter @readi/api content:review-doc                         # the pages the expert reads
```

**A local caveat, not a defect:** the dry run reports eight rows as "left alone — published" on a
developer machine where they were published by hand. ADR-0014 decision 7 working as designed; a fresh
database takes everything, `-- --force` is the way through on a developer one.
