# M4 phase 4 — the report the candidate reads

**Branch `feat/m4-evaluation`. 2026-09-27.** Phase 3 scored sessions and stored reports that nothing
served. This phase serves them: a route, a screen, and the two recoveries a lost enqueue needs.

`tasks/todo.md` carries the checklist. This is what a reader needs that the checklist does not say.

## The screen

`/interview/:id/report`, in the `(session)` group — it belongs to the interview it is about, and the
navigation is not what somebody wants halfway through reading their score. **No client JavaScript on
the route**: the report is text, some bars and some links, and the waiting belongs to the completion
screen. A candidate whose report is not ready is redirected there, which is where all three of "not
finished", "still scoring" and "you answered nothing" are explained.

The Margin identity (ADR-0013) is a page of the candidate's own work with a mentor's notes beside it,
and a report is the first screen where that is literally what is on offer. So:

- the transcript's voice grammar is kept exactly — the question is the serif at full measure, the
  candidate's own words are the sans on a ruled rail;
- each criterion is a `Margined` pair: the dimension, the score and the **verified quote** on the left,
  the evaluator's reasoning as a `Note` on the right at `lg` and underneath at 360px. That pairing is
  the one place the identity and the data model agree exactly, because a quote and the reasoning about
  it are what the evaluator actually produced together;
- the quotes are `Highlight`s (a real `<mark>`), with **no numbers**. ADR-0013 numbers the quotes on
  the landing page because there the phrases are embedded in running prose and the notes are in a
  separate column; here every quote already sits beside the one note that explains it.

**The score card is a card because it is the thing somebody will screenshot** (the owner's decision 8:
no public share URL at MVP, and a screenshot is the honest substitute). So it has to read cropped to
its own border, and it carries **no quote from the transcript**. The band is a word beside the number
and never instead of it, and the note under it says what the number is not — readiness needs more than
one interview and is M6's.

### Two copy decisions worth arguing with

**"Before you were asked" is second person and impersonal.** The owner's decision 6 phrased it "you
covered two of the three points before I asked", but the product says "we" everywhere else and never
"I" outside the interviewer's own turns. It is now "You brought up 2 of 3 points without being asked"
and "One question needed a follow-up", which loses the mentor's first person and keeps the product's
voice. The copy is a draft for the owner, like the landing page's.

**The lessons are a reading list, not links.** There is no candidate-facing lesson page in the app
yet, and a title that looks like a link and answers 404 is worse than one that admits what it is. The
query is still worth running: naming the lesson a content expert wrote for the topic somebody went
worst on is most of its value, and the day the page exists this becomes a list of links with no other
change. Five of eight role × level pairs have no published track at all, so the *other* empty state is
the one most reports will show.

## The contract grew three fields, and each is a rule made structural

**`strengths` and `fixes` carry the question they came from** (`ReportHighlight`). They are chosen in
code from the answers that went best and worst, so every one of them is a claim about one specific
answer — and without saying which, "say what you measured" is advice a candidate cannot check. The
summary now links to the question's own section, where its quotes are.

They carry a **position, not a quote**, and that was the harder call. Pairing a session-level tip with
a criterion-level quote would assert a link nothing made: the evaluator paired *its* quotes with *its*
reasoning, per criterion, and a plausible-looking mis-pairing is worse feedback than none. So the
summary attributes and the breakdown quotes.

**The report carries the pinned catalogue** (`role`, `level`, `stack` from `interview_sessions.
catalogue`). CLAUDE.md's rule is that renaming a role must not rewrite a report the candidate has
already read; joining the live rows at read time would have broken it on the one surface where the
name is actually printed. **And `ended_at`**, because a report the sweep recovers days later must not
date itself by its own assembly.

## The two recoveries, and the bug that made one of them a no-op

Phase 3 left this: the enqueue happens after the SSE stream closes and is logged rather than raised
(ADR-0016 forbids a throw in there), so it can be lost, and **nothing else would notice** — the stale
sweep reads `in_progress` sessions only.

- **Opening the report queues one.** `GET /api/interviews/:id/report` answers `report_not_ready` (409)
  after queueing, and the completion screen polls through it. Free when the answers are already stored.
- **`EvaluationSweepQueue` is the half that does not wait for anybody to look**, every ten minutes, on
  the `StaleSessionsQueue` pattern. A candidate who never opens their report would otherwise go
  unscored for good, and that is not merely a missing page: `weakTopics` reads `answer_evaluations`, so
  it quietly degrades the *next* interview, and M6's readiness score is computed from stored scores,
  where a gap is a wrong number rather than a blank one.

**The sweep's query is one predicate, and the trap answered itself.** The worry was paying for the same
refusal every ten minutes for ever. It cannot happen, structurally rather than by a special case:
`assemble()` stores a `failed` report even when nothing could be scored, so a refused session leaves
the query for good; and an answer that has a row is never re-scored, so a session that stored answers
but not its report re-assembles for **no model call at all**. The only thing the sweep can spend money
on is a session that was never evaluated. It is bounded anyway — oldest first, twenty at a time, with
fifteen minutes' grace — because a day of lost enqueues found at once should drain over several sweeps.

**And the recovery did not work the first time.** The job id is the session id, so two doors closing at
once enqueue one job. But BullMQ keeps completed jobs (`removeOnComplete: { count: 1000 }`) and
`Queue.add` with an existing id **silently returns the existing job**, so re-queueing a session it had
already scored did nothing: the route answered "still being scored" for ever and the sweep queued into
a void. `enqueue` now removes a **completed or failed** job under that id first and leaves a waiting,
delayed or active one alone — which is where the dedupe was always meant to be. The route's own test
found it.

## The leak test learned a second narrowing

M3 taught `content-no-answer-key.int.spec.ts` that a planned follow-up may appear inside a turn an
interviewer has spoken. The report is the second surface with a moment when part of the key is allowed
out, and it is narrowed in the same shape rather than excused.

The spec now runs a **real, scored session** — through the engine and the real queue — and asserts three
separate claims, because any one alone would pass for the wrong reason:

1. the unconditional key (criterion descriptions, weights, all five level descriptors) is absent;
2. the pinned ideal points appear **exactly as many times** as `strong_answer_covers` shows them, so one
   smuggled into a `reasoning` string fails;
3. a dimension appears only as a criterion's `dimension`, and nowhere else.

The detector gained `allowKeys` for the three field names a report legitimately has (`criteria`,
`criteria_total`, `criteria_volunteered`), with a control proving the allowance is doing something *and*
that a grafted rubric still fails with it in place. `content-fixtures.ts` gained `idealPointMarkers`
and `dimensionMarkers` as **subsets** of `answerKeyMarkers` rather than replacements —
`interviews-advance.int.spec.ts` asserts the worker's bundle carries none of the key, and that claim has
to keep covering ideal points and dimensions.

`interview.spec.ts` makes the same claim one layer out, on the rendered page: a component is perfectly
capable of rendering a field the route never sent.

## Two things found on the way

**A Playwright click with no action timeout hangs rather than fails.** `capture.spec.ts` was changed to
click "See where you got to" on its way to the completion screen. The interview screen navigates itself
(`router.replace`), so the link detaches — and with no `actionTimeout` in `playwright.config.ts`,
Playwright retried the click until the *test* timeout. Twenty minutes of the spec producing nothing.
The M3 comment beside that link already warned that clicking it races the redirect. Wait for the URL.

**The sweep's first test asserted "nothing was swept" and failed** against sessions other tests in the
same file had left behind — it reads the whole database, and every spec in the suite shares one. It
returns ids now, and the assertions name their own session.

## Honest states, all of them on purpose

A failed answer gets its own frame, says the failure is ours, is left out of the number rather than
counted as nothing, and **still shows what a strong answer covers** — the useful half of a lost score.
A session where nothing could be scored shows no number at all. A session nobody answered is never
polled for, because the transcript already says there is nothing to score. Scoring that outlives three
minutes stops polling and says so: spec §8 wants a report in 60 s, and a spinner that outlives its job
is the failure that bound exists to prevent.
