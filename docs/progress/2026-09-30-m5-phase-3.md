## The five decisions in this phase that are decisions, not implementation

**The session is given no language model.** `AgentSession(stt=…, tts=…, vad=…)` and **no `llm`**:
LiveKit then runs the transport — streaming recognition, endpointing, barge-in, transcription into
the room, reconnection — calls `Agent.on_user_turn_completed` and stops, because it has no reply to
generate. `session.generate_reply()` raises, which is the right failure for a call nothing in this
package should ever make. That is ADR-0019 §1's "voice is a transport" as an invariant rather than a
convention: there is no second state machine, and nowhere for one to hide.

**A probe counts as asked only if the candidate heard its ask** (owner's decision, 2026-09-29).
A turn talked over before its ask is pushed with `follow_up_index: null`, so the criterion lands in
M4's `unaskedCriteria` and is **not assessed** — the clock rule of 2026-09-27 in another transport,
because a candidate never loses marks for something they did not hear. Three boundaries, each
deliberate: the engine's `probes_asked` still holds the probe (so the cap counts it and it is not
re-asked — the candidate's act spends it and does not score them); how far playback got is
**measured**, from LiveKit's `synchronized_transcript` where a room session gives one and otherwise
from the played position mapped onto the text with one clipped syllable of tolerance; and anything
less certain than that counts as **not heard**, because the two errors are not equal.

**Lever 2 fires only where the answer cannot change what happens next.** `settled_next_step` returns
a step only when `probes_to_judge` is empty — the follow-up budget spent, every probe asked or
covered, or no room for one — and each of those is monotone in the direction that keeps it sound:
time runs down, the cap never loosens, coverage only removes probes from play. So the prefetch is
prefetching and not speculation. The phrasing is made by the **service**, through the same renderer
the turn uses, and cached on the rendered prompt, so a prefetch that misses is one wasted call and
never a second copy of the question prompt — which is the mistake `review-doc.ts` made in M3 and
the one this was most at risk of repeating.

**The push waits for the turn's audio to settle.** ADR-0019 §3 forbids a database write between the
model's answer and the first audio byte, and none happens — but `spoken_ms` and `interrupted` do not
exist until the audio stops, and §8 is the reason they have to be recorded. Pushes are also
serialised: two in flight could apply an older engine snapshot over a newer one, and the snapshot is
what the next leg resumes from.

**Cost rows for the live path come from LiveKit's own metrics.** `SpeechMeter` listens to
`STTMetrics`/`TTSMetrics` rather than wrapping a plugin, so nothing on that path can happen without
an `AiCallRecord` and no plugin's internals have to be re-implemented to bill it. A
**session-billed** vendor (AssemblyAI streaming bills the socket, not the audio) produces **one**
record when the socket closes, priced on the socket's lifetime; an audio-billed one produces one per
recognition. The units column stays the audio either way. **LiveKit Inference is deliberately not
used**: it would reach the same vendors through LiveKit's gateway at rates `speech/pricing.py` does
not hold, and an unpriceable call is the one thing the owner's instruction of 2026-09-29 forbids.

## What was proved against the LiveKit dev server, and what was not

Run, with the compose stack up:

```bash
VOICE_ENABLED=true LIVEKIT_URL=ws://127.0.0.1:7880 LIVEKIT_API_KEY=devkey \
LIVEKIT_API_SECRET=secret pnpm dev:voice
```

- the agent **registers** as `readi-interviewer` (explicit dispatch, so it joins no room it was not
  sent to);
- a dispatch carrying `{"session_id": "…"}` **reaches the entrypoint**, and the id is read from the
  dispatch metadata alone — the bundle is pulled over the service-token channel, because room
  metadata is readable by participants and `planned_follow_ups` are answer key (ADR-0019 §4);
- the pull then **fails cleanly**, because the API's internal routes are phase 4: one log line
  naming the error code, no traceback, and a job that ends rather than crashes.

**Nothing has run end to end through a room**, and it cannot until phase 4's routes and phase 5's
browser exist. What stands in for that today is the 25 leg tests: a whole leg driven through the
`transport.py` Protocols with a speaker that records lines and answers with scripted playback —
the greeting, an exchange, a barge-in, the silence prompt, the prefetch, an early end, the
allowance, a lost push. WSL's NAT makes local *timing* meaningless in any case (ADR-0001), which is
why phase 8 exists.

## Two bugs the tests found, both in the mapping rather than the logic

**A `TimedString` with no offsets carries `NOT_GIVEN`, not `None`.** The first version of
`_words_of` checked for None, so a provider that timed one word loosely would have raised inside the
recognition stream — one word's worth of delivery coaching against a whole leg. It is a type check
now, and the test that found it is the one asserting a word with no offsets is dropped rather than
invented.

**The barge-in tolerance was written the wrong way round.** It was a margin the candidate had to
hear *beyond* the ask, which made a question played to its very last character count as unheard
whenever they interrupted at the end — which is the commonest barge-in there is. It is a tolerance
*towards* the ask now: one clipped syllable, four characters, and a probe cut off mid-sentence is
nowhere near it.

## Carried into phase 4

- **The three internal routes.** `voice/api_client.py` is written against the contracts and driven
  through a mock transport; phase 4 builds the other side and the first real leg becomes possible.
- **A resumed leg says nothing and waits**, which is what `VoiceSessionStartResponse` documents —
  but on a voice reconnection the candidate heard nothing of the question they are now expected to
  answer. The captions carry it (phase 5), so silence costs them a read rather than a repetition;
  re-speaking would need the API to send the last interviewer turn, which the contract does not
  carry. Worth deciding with the captions in front of you.
- **RTT and packet loss are null on `VoiceQuality`.** They would have to come out of
  `room.get_rtc_stats()`'s WebRTC objects, and the measurement that decides the region is phase 8
  stage 1 — the candidate's *browser* reporting `getStats()` over MTN, Airtel, Glo and home
  broadband. `QualityMonitor.note()` already takes them, so phase 8 adds a call and not a design.
- **An unheard *question* is asked again** — added on 2026-09-30, after the phase, and described in
  its own section below. What is left of that gap is the case past the cap.
- **`VoiceLegEndReason` gained `allowance_exhausted`**, so phase 4's ledger has a word for the leg
  the agent closed before crossing the session's remaining voice seconds.

## Added after the phase: a question talked over is asked again (2026-09-30)

The owner's follow-up on phase 3's known gap: if a candidate talks over the **opening question**
before its ask, the interviewer should finish or repeat it rather than score an answer to a question
they never heard — the probe rule's principle, one turn earlier. It is built, and it landed in phase
3 rather than 4 because none of it is API work: it is the leg and one pure rule beside the probe
rule.

**"Finish" was rejected on the source, and that is the interesting part.** Holding the interruption
until the ask has been spoken reads better — the interviewer simply finishes its sentence — and
LiveKit supports it (`allow_interruptions=False`, flipped once playback passes the ask). But a
candidate turn that commits while the current speech cannot be interrupted is **dropped entirely**:
`agent_activity.py:2774-2779` logs a warning and returns *before* `on_user_turn_completed` runs, so
the driver never sees it. The failure mode is therefore a candidate answering and being unheard,
which is a worse bug than the one being fixed and a silent one. `discard_audio_if_uninterruptible:
False` does not help: it keeps the audio flowing to the recogniser, so the words reach the captions
and are then thrown away at the same `return`.

**So: repeat.** In `voice/barge_in.py`, `needs_repeating` is the rule (a *question* whose ask was not
heard, never a probe) and `merged` is how two attempts become one turn. In `session.py`:

- the repeat costs **no model call** — the same words, and the same pinned or prefetched audio where
  there is any — so it cannot rephrase the question into a different one;
- **words that ended before the question did are not an answer to it.** The interjection the
  candidate made over it commits a moment after the repeat starts; taking it would score "sorry,
  what?" and waste the repeat. Dropped, with a log line and no transcript;
- **one repeat per question.** Past the cap their words are taken as the answer, because a candidate
  who talks over everything must not spend the session on one question and be scored on nothing;
- the turn is recorded **`interrupted` with the second attempt's `spoken_ms`**: they did talk over it
  (a delivery fact M6 will want) and they did, in the end, hear the whole question. The first
  attempt's own position is in the leg's log and nowhere else, which is the one thing this loses.

**Only a question, never a probe.** The probe rule already protects the candidate from being scored
on one they did not hear, and re-asking something they deliberately talked over would be an
interviewer who had not noticed. A question is different: there is nothing else for the answer to be
about.

Two things this leaves, both in `tasks/todo.md` for phase 5: the captions will show a dropped
interjection that appears in no turn of the stored transcript (LiveKit transcribes the candidate into
the room independently of us), and past the cap an unheard question is still scored.
