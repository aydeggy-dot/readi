## Why this exists

Readi asks candidates to answer interview questions in their own words. A candidate's answers are
personal data: they describe where that person works, what they have built, what they do not yet know,
and how they handle being stuck.

To know whether our AI scores those answers **fairly**, a human being has to score some of the same
answers independently, without seeing what the AI said, so the two can be compared. That is what a
calibration reviewer does, and it is the only honest way to make the claim we make to candidates —
that our feedback is fair — testable rather than asserted.

A candidate is asked for permission before any answer of theirs can be sampled, and can refuse or
withdraw at any time, with no effect on what they get from the product. **This agreement is the other
half of that promise**: the candidate has been told a person may read their answer to check our
scoring, and nothing more than that. This document is what keeps that true.

## Who the parties are

- **Readi** (the controller — the company decides why and how candidate data is processed).
- **You, the reviewer** (a processor — you process candidate data only on Readi's instructions, for
  the purpose set out below, and for no purpose of your own).

You are engaged as an independent contractor, paid per review. Nothing here makes you an employee.

## What you may do with candidate answers

**One thing only: read a sampled answer and score it against the rubric shown beside it, inside the
Readi calibration tool.**

That is the entire permitted purpose. It follows that you may **not**:

1. **Copy it.** Do not paste an answer, or any part of one, into another document, a notes app, a
   spreadsheet, a chat, an email, a code editor, an issue tracker, or a model or AI assistant of any
   kind. Do not screenshot it. Do not print it. Score inside the tool.
2. **Share it.** Not with a colleague, a friend, a client, another reviewer, a candidate, a recruiter,
   an employer, or on social media — not the answer, not a quote from it, not a paraphrase, and not a
   description specific enough to identify who wrote it.
3. **Keep it.** You are not asked to store anything, and you should not. If you end up with a local
   copy of any candidate answer — a download, a screenshot, an accidental paste, a browser file —
   delete it as soon as you notice, and tell us.
4. **Use it for anything else.** Not to write interview questions, not as an example in teaching or a
   talk, not to train or evaluate a model of your own, not for research, not to recruit, and not to
   form or share a view about the person who wrote it outside the score you give.
5. **Try to find out who wrote it.** See the next section.

## Blind review: what you will and will not see

You will see the question, the rubric, and the candidate's answer.

You will **not** see the candidate's name, email address, phone number, CV, employer, photograph, or
account. The tool identifies an answer by an internal id, on purpose.

If an answer happens to contain something identifying — candidates sometimes name their employer, a
product, or a colleague while explaining their work — you must:

- **not** act on it, look it up, or repeat it;
- score the answer as written, treating the detail as part of the answer and nothing more;
- flag it to us if it looks like something the candidate would not have meant to include.

You must not attempt to identify a candidate by any route: not by searching a phrase from the answer,
not by matching it against a CV or a profile you have seen elsewhere, and not by asking us.

**If you recognise the answer** — because you know the person, or you interviewed them, or you believe
you wrote part of the code they are describing — stop, do not score it, and tell us. A conflict of
interest is not a failure on your part; scoring through one is.

## Confidentiality

Everything you see in the tool is confidential: candidate answers, and also our questions, our rubrics
and our scoring criteria, which are unpublished work product. The obligation does not end when a
review ends and does not end when the engagement ends. It continues indefinitely.

Readi's own materials are confidential for a second reason worth stating plainly: a rubric that
circulated would let candidates score well without being ready, which would harm the candidates who
rely on our feedback being real.

## Access, and the record of it

- Your access is personal. Do not share your account, your password or your session with anyone.
- Use a device you control, with a screen lock and full-disk encryption if you have it. Do not review
  on a shared or public computer.
- **Which answers you opened is logged.** This is deliberate and it is not distrust: it is how we can
  tell a candidate honestly who has seen their answer, and it is how you are protected if a question
  is ever raised about access you did not make.
- Your access is granted for the work and withdrawn when the work ends, or sooner at our discretion or
  yours.

## Deletion

- You are not expected to hold any candidate data, so in the normal case there is nothing to delete.
- If you do hold any — see point 3 above — delete it as soon as you notice.
- **When the engagement ends**, confirm in writing that you hold no candidate answers, notes quoting
  them, screenshots, or exports, in any location including cloud storage, backups, email and chat
  history.
- If we ask you to delete something specific, do it promptly and confirm.

## If something goes wrong

Tell us **as soon as you know** — same day. Do not wait until you have worked out how bad it is, and do
not try to fix it quietly first. This includes: an answer sent to the wrong person, a device lost or
stolen, an account compromised, a paste into the wrong window, or a suspicion that any of these has
happened.

We would far rather hear about a near miss early than discover a real one late, and reporting promptly
is treated as doing the right thing, not as an admission.

> *A data-protection law imposes its own breach-notification deadlines on Readi, which is why "same
> day" matters. The lawyer reviewing this should set out the exact obligation and any consequence of
> failing it.*

## Your own obligations to the law

You will comply with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and any other data-protection law that
applies to your work for us. You will not engage anyone else to help you with a review — no
sub-processing, including no assistant, no colleague and no AI service — without our written
agreement first.

## Left for the lawyer

These are deliberately not drafted here, because guessing at them would be worse than leaving them
blank:

- Governing law and jurisdiction.
- Term, termination and survival of the confidentiality obligation.
- Liability, indemnity and insurance.
- The statutory content a processor agreement must carry under the NDPA 2023 (and the GDPR's Article
  28 list, if it applies), including audit and assistance obligations.
- Breach-notification timing expressed as an obligation on the reviewer rather than a request.
- Whether anything here needs to differ for a reviewer based outside Nigeria, and what a cross-border
  transfer of candidate data to that reviewer requires.
- Consequences of breach, and whether any part of this needs to be a separate NDA.
